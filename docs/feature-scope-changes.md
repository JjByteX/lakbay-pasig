# Pasig Tourism App: From / To Changes

## 1. Overall Positioning

**From:** A directory of places in Pasig with reviews, categories, and self-listing, similar to a smaller Google Maps.

**To:** Pasig City's own platform for telling its own story, kept current by the Tourism Office, turning a walk around the city into a guided experience instead of a search result.

## 2. Historical and Cultural Content

**From:** Historical content developed once with the Tourism Office and professors, added at launch.

**To:** An ongoing content pipeline where the Tourism Office pushes updates, corrections, and new content directly into the app, with a "recently updated" marker. Every fact is labeled "Verified by Pasig Tourism Office" or with a named contributor.

This is not hypothetical. CATO already runs real, ongoing public programs. In May 2026 they launched the Pasig Creative Arts Academy, offering seven free courses to 284 young Pasigueños, with the Acting Mayor and CATO's OIC both present. CATO's scope is also expanding, a Youth Development Center in Barangay Maybunga was placed under their supervision by executive order in 2026. This means there is already a real stream of content to plug into, not a stream that needs to be invented from scratch.

Right now, this kind of announcement lives scattered across Facebook and the general city news feed, mixed in with unrelated items like bid openings and weather warnings. A parent looking for enrollment info for something like PCAA has to dig through flood warnings to find it. Consolidating tourism and culture announcements into the app, separated from general city business, is a specific and provable value the app can offer immediately.

## 3. Proximity "Secrets" Feature

**From:** Standalone trivia facts unlocked when near a location.

**To:** Sequenced stories, where each stop in a heritage walk references the last one and sets up the next, turning individual facts into a narrative arc instead of disconnected pop ups.

CATO already runs guided photo walks in person, documented on their Facebook page, showing groups being led through Pasig's culture and history by CATO staff. This means the sequenced heritage walk concept is not an invented idea, it mirrors something the office already does physically. Worth asking the office directly whether the app could host schedules, routes, or documentation for these existing walks, instead of designing new walks independently from scratch.

## 4. Vendor Listings

**From:** Any business can self-list, sits as a generic listing similar to a Google Business Profile.

**To:** Two tiers. Basic self-listing stays open to any vendor. A separate "featured in official food crawl or heritage walk" status exists for vendors selected as part of a curated food crawl or heritage walk, using language like "part of the official Pasig food crawl" that vendors can use on their own storefront. Selection works as a direct staff toggle, similar to pinning a post, no formal criteria required, CATO decides at their own discretion.

## 5. Vendor Dashboard

**From:** Simple dashboard showing views and saves.

**To:** Metrics tied to itinerary inclusion, such as "included in 3 active food crawls" or "part of 140 completed heritage walks this month," which ties the dashboard back to the app's actual differentiator instead of generic analytics Google already provides.

## 6. Gamification

**From:** Badges and points for visiting places or unlocking facts.

**To:** A credential for completing a specific named route, which feels earned rather than a generic point counter.

## 7. Rankings

**From:** Leaderboard style ranking of how many times someone visited a place.

**To:** No individual repeat-visit leaderboard. Use cohort framing instead, such as "one of 200 people who completed this route this month," or rank by barangay or neighborhood instead of by individual person.

## 8. Business Value Proposition

**From:** Not clearly defined beyond visibility in the app itself.

**To:** Resolved. The offer is the visibility itself, being included in a Tourism Office backed directory that residents and tourists already check regularly. This does not depend on CATO providing anything beyond app inclusion, such as social media mentions or printed materials. Being part of a CATO backed directory is a real differentiator on its own, since it carries institutional credibility no unaffiliated directory, including Google, can offer. External perks were considered but are not needed for the core value proposition to hold.

## 9. Institutional Backing as Evidence

**From:** Stated as a partnership claim without supporting detail.

**To:** Backed by the office's legal mandate under Ordinance No. SP-17, Series of 1998, and by documented bottlenecks in their own Citizen's Charter, specifically a 7 working day process for requesting cultural or tourism data, and a 40 day process for implementing a tourism event. The app's value is framed as shortening a real, provable process rather than a vague claim of helping tourism.

This is backed further by the office's actual activity in 2026, including the Pasig Creative Arts Academy launch, expanded oversight of the Youth Development Center, and regular guided photo walks. CATO is an active, growing office with real public programs, not a dormant department signing a partnership letter for the sake of it.

## 10. The Filter for Every Feature

**From:** No consistent test for what belongs in the pitch.

**To:** Ask whether Google could copy the feature without the Tourism Office relationship. If yes, it is infrastructure, not a differentiator. Build it if needed, but do not lead with it in the pitch.

## Resolved After Meeting With CATO

- Old system database: does not exist for CATO to share, the previous third-party developer left with it. App starts fresh on all content, no migration needed.
- Talking Walls (QR codes at physical locations): removed after the previous developer left, no longer exist. Any QR-based unlock feature would be new infrastructure, not integration with something already in place.
- Feasibility study: CATO does not need to be convinced, they already want the system. Feasibility forms for tourists, residents, and vendors are still worth running for capstone data, but the CATO staff form shifts from persuasion to confirming operational details.
- Ordinance No. 39-2023: confirmed not needed, dropped from scope.
- Featured status process: CATO selects vendors entirely on their own initiative, outside the app. No in-app request or application flow exists. A vendor who wants to be considered contacts CATO through a separate channel.
- Vendor value proposition: resolved as app visibility alone, no external perks (social media mentions, printed materials, event priority) required or promised.
- Past vendor list: confirmed, CATO has a list of vendors from past food fairs and events. Not yet requested or imported, deferred until after core build, since it is a content task, not a design or architecture decision.
- Featured status criteria: no formal criteria needed. Featured is a direct staff action, similar to pinning a post. CATO staff views an existing listing and marks it Featured at their own discretion, no checklist or scoring required.
