# Pasig Tourism App: What To Actually Do

## The One Line Pitch

Google Maps tells tourists what is nearby. This app is Pasig City's own platform for telling its own story, kept current by the Tourism Office, turning a walk around the city into a guided experience instead of a plain search result.

## Do These Five Things

**1. Label everything as verified.**
Every fact or story shows "Verified by Pasig Tourism Office" or a named contributor. Trust signal Google Reviews cannot match.

**2. Build a real content pipeline, not a one time upload.**
Let the Tourism Office push updates, corrections, and event info directly into the app. Show a "recently updated" marker. This is what keeps the app alive after launch.

**3. Turn heritage walks into sequenced stories, not trivia pop ups.**
Each stop should reference the last one and set up the next. This is what proximity unlocks should actually be for.

**4. Frame vendors as part of a curated route, not a listing.**
"Part of the official Pasig food crawl" is worth more to a small business than "5 restaurants nearby." Give vendors that language to use on their own storefronts.

**5. Make the office actually run on it, not just endorse it.**
Push for event schedules, route updates, and foot traffic data to flow through the app. This is the difference between an app the city approved and an app the city depends on.

## Use This as Proof, Not Just a Claim

Pasig's own Citizen's Charter shows:
- Requesting cultural or tourism data from the office takes up to **7 working days**
- Running a full tourism event takes up to **40 days**, through multiple rounds of internal approval

If the app shortcuts either process, that is a specific, provable claim for the capstone defense, not a vague "we help tourism."

## Skip or Downsize

- **Badges and points:** replace with a credential for completing a specific route. Feels earned, not generic.
- **Visit count leaderboards:** do not do individual repeat visit rankings. Small user base makes it look empty, and it rewards the wrong behavior. Use cohort stats instead, like "one of 200 people who finished this route this month," or rank by barangay instead of by person.
- **Self listing for vendors:** keep it, but treat it as plumbing, not a selling point.
- **Bilingual support:** keep it, do not market it as a feature.

## The Test for Every Feature

Can Google copy this without the Tourism Office relationship? If yes, it is not a differentiator, it is just infrastructure. Build it, but do not lead with it.
